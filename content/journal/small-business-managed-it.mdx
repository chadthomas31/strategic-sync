If you've got a handful of computers and no IT person, you've probably asked: do I really need to pay every month, or just call someone when something breaks?

Both are valid. Here's how to tell which fits.

## Break-fix is fine when…

- You have just a few computers
- A little downtime isn't a big deal
- You don't store sensitive client data
- Problems are rare and you'd rather pay as they come

For a lot of small shops, hourly support ($95/hr, onsite or remote) is exactly right.

## Managed IT pays off when…

- Downtime costs you real money or clients
- You handle sensitive data (health, legal, financial)
- You've been burned by lost files or no backup
- You'd rather problems get caught *before* they take you down

A flat monthly plan means we **monitor** your computers and network, run **automated backups** and **security updates**, and give you **priority support** — so the goal shifts from fixing emergencies to preventing them.

## What managed IT actually covers

- Proactive monitoring of every machine and your network
- Automated, tested backups (the #1 thing that saves businesses)
- Managed antivirus / endpoint protection
- Security patches and updates handled for you
- Priority same-day support and a quarterly tech check-in

## The honest answer

If you're a two-person office that mostly uses email and a browser, break-fix is plenty. If your business stops when the computers stop — or you're handling data you can't afford to lose — managed IT is cheaper than one bad day.
